### Teardown of the detector

A specimen of the artifact detector known among stalkers as the "Medved" (Bear) was handed over to the research group in an informal exchange with a lone stalker on MBU grounds. The device was in working order, with its factory markings partly missing. Considerable help with the study came from a technician from the stalker community who wished to remain anonymous; he took part in the full teardown of the device and explained a number of non-standard engineering solutions that a superficial inspection would not reveal.

Unlike serial detectors of the "Otklik" (Echo) type, the Medved was not originally built merely to indicate that an artifact is present, but as a search and analysis tool combining the functions of an anomaly detector, an artifact locator and a radiation dosimeter. ### Design features and 3D reconstruction

After a full teardown, the detector was divided into functional modules:

* a sensor unit (electromagnetic and ultrasonic),

* a computing module with a microprocessor,

* a display and control unit,

* a housing originally used for a "Pripyat"-type Geiger counter.

### Operating principle and modes

The Medved detector has several operating modes:

1. ***Closed state (passive mode)***    With the lid closed, the device works as an anomaly and radiation detector: the antenna registers the general radiation background (built-in Geiger counter) and records anomalous electromagnetic disturbances, without trying to locate a specific artifact.

2. ***Artifact search mode***    When the antenna lid is raised, the directional locator is activated. In this mode the device picks up the artifact's return signal, shows its direction and approximate distance, and automatically selects the nearest anomalous formation.

3. ***Active interaction mode***    At minimum distance, the Medved can generate a brief pulse (EMP/"relic" radiation) that disrupts the stability of the anomalous structure and causes the artifact to materialise.

### Advantages over the Otklik detector

A comparative analysis established that the Medved is significantly superior to the Otklik in the following respects:

* **Detection bandwidth**:   The Otklik works mainly in a narrow low-frequency band, while the Medved covers the spectrum from infrasound to ultrasound and registers rare electromagnetic signatures of artifacts.

* **Directionality**:   The Otklik gives no spatial information, while the Medved shows direction and distance, reducing the risk of walking into a dangerous anomaly.

* **Interference filtering**:   A built-in spectrum analyser and processing algorithms make it possible to tell artifact signals apart from background radiation and industrial sources.

* **Versatility**:   Combining an artifact detector and a dosimeter makes the device especially valuable for scientific expeditions and the collection of anomalous samples.

### Scientific value for research

Using the Medved detector opens up new possibilities:

* building a database of artifact spectral signatures,
* studying the mechanisms of their materialisation,
* researching the interaction of electromagnetic, acoustic and anomalous fields,
* improving the safety of field work.

In effect, the Medved can be regarded as a transitional link between stalker equipment and a fully fledged scientific instrument, created in the conditions of the CAZ yet meeting the requirements of systematic anomaly analysis.

### Conclusion

The development and subsequent study of the Medved detector showed that even in the informal stalker environment, engineering solutions superior to outdated serial models are possible. Taking all its characteristics together, the Medved is recognised as a promising tool for further scientific research into artifacts and anomalous fields and is recommended for adaptation to the needs of NIIChAZ, with subsequent standardisation of its design.
