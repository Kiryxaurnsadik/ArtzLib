### Background to the development

Operating the KDA-7 container showed that, despite its high level of protection, its form factor and weight limit the use of artifacts directly on the wearer. At the same time, stalker practice showed a steady demand for devices that allow the positive properties of anomalous energy to be used safely, without direct contact with the body.

This led to the development of the SKA-1, a compact wearable system that is a scaled-down and rethought version of the KDA-7, adapted to small anomalous formations.

### General concept of the SKA-1

The SKA-1 is a belt-mounted platform equipped with:

* A battery-powered supply system.
* Small protective containers.
* A field shielding system.

Unlike standard containers, the SKA-1 is not meant for transport but for actively wearing and using artifacts: the negative effects of anomalous energy are isolated, while the useful ones are passed on to the wearer in a weakened and stabilised form.

### Structural base

The base used was the stalker belt from the "Zarya" kit, chosen for the following reasons:

* High resistance of the material to background radiation.
* Proven ergonomics for long-term wear.
* A reliable system of fastenings and load distribution.
* Minimal effect on the user's mobility.

The belt was modified with reinforced mounting points and integrated cable channels.

### The KDAS-1S and KDA-7 containers in the development of the SKA-1

The SKA-1 uses scaled-down KDAS-1S containers, but with KDA-7 electronics optimised for small and medium-sized artifacts.

The SKA-1 containers feature:

* Multilayer shielding against radiation and psi radiation.
* Suppression of the harmful effects of anomalous energy.
* Directed filtering of useful properties.
* Full isolation of the artifact from direct contact with the body.

The containers are placed symmetrically, which reduces uneven loading and field distortions.

### Container power system

The SKA-1 has a built-in power system that provides:

* Operation of the shielding circuits.
* Stabilisation of anomalous energy.
* Power for the internal sensors.

When power is available, the containers switch to active mode, in which:

* The artifact's negative properties are absorbed and dispersed,
* Positive effects are passed on to the wearer in a safe form.

When power is cut, the containers automatically switch to passive protective mode, shutting down the anomalous energy filtering systems.

### Protective properties of the containers

**SKA-1**

* Does not let through background radiation from artifacts placed in the 2 containers on the belt.
* Blocks degradation of the biofield.
* Prevents the build-up of anomalous stress in the body.

It should be stressed that the system does not amplify artifacts; it only allows their normal positive effects to be used safely.

### Limitations and drawbacks

The recorded limitations include:

* Poorer thermal insulation because of the cut-outs for the containers.
* Dependence on power supply.
* Restrictions on the types and power of artifacts.
* Inability to work with unstable or "wild" formations.

### Conclusion

The SKA-1 is a logical development of the concept of individual work with artifacts. Unlike heavy storage containers, the system makes it possible to integrate anomalous formations into a person's equipment without exposing them to a direct threat.
