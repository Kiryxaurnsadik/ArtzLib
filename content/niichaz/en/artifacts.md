An anomalous formation (hereafter Artifact\*) is a product of anomalous fields. According to the working hypothesis, an artifact is a stabilised material carrier of an anomalous process. Its properties are directly linked to the type of anomaly. The material of the object that fell into the anomaly has the same kind of influence.

Conditions for the formation of anomalous formations:
1. A long stay of an object (organic or inorganic) in an active anomaly.
2. Restructuring of its crystalline and/or molecular structure under the action of anomalous fields and the anomalous processes within them.
3. Ejection of the object during the energy discharge phase of the anomaly (most often after an anomalous storm).

### Initial state of artifacts

At the moment of removal from the anomalous environment, the vast majority of artifacts are classified as "Unknown". In this state the artifact:

* does not show the full range of its properties,
* may show unstable or false effects,
* is a potential danger to the operator.

This is because the anomalous process inside the artifact is in a latent phase and requires external diagnostics for correct identification.

### Analysis of artifact quality

Using the USI-5 (Universal Artifact Research Station, 5th generation series) makes it possible to determine an artifact's real quality. Based on a combination of parameters (energy density, field stability, degree of spatial distortion), artifacts are divided into the following categories:

* **Legendary**
  Exceptionally rare specimens with a highly stable anomalous core. They have unique or previously unknown properties, often beyond current theoretical models.
* **Rare**
  Artifacts with pronounced and stable effects exceeding standard parameters. They often show enhanced versions of known properties.
* **Unusual**
  Specimens with an atypical field configuration. Their properties may be specific, situational or appear only under certain conditions.
* **Common**
  The most widespread category. Properties are stable, well studied and reproducible.
* **Junk**
  Degraded or defective formations. The anomalous core is unstable or destroyed; useful properties are absent or minimal.

It is important to note that quality correlates directly with the depth and stability of the anomalous process the object went through at the moment of formation.

### Discovery of new properties

In a number of cases, analysing an artifact reveals hidden or improved properties that were not recorded before:

* a change in the intensity of the effect,
* the appearance of secondary but controllable anomalous reactions,
* interaction with other artifacts or fields.

This effect is especially typical of artifacts in the *Rare* and *Legendary* categories.

### Theory of the wave signature of artifacts
According to the professor's working hypothesis, each artifact has its own wave signature, formed by:

* the type of anomaly that produced it,
* the material of the original object,
* the degree of stabilisation of the anomalous process.

This signature is a combination of:

* electromagnetic,
* gravitational,
* psi-information oscillations.

It is by these waves that artifacts are detected.

### Energy of artifacts
Artifacts, despite the stability of their anomalous properties, are not sources of infinite energy. During use, a gradual depletion of their inner anomalous core is recorded, showing up as a weakening or temporary disappearance of their effects. This is because the artifact uses up the energy potential accumulated during its formation and cannot replenish it on its own outside an anomalous environment. To restore it to working order, the KSZA-3M device (Compact Artifact Charging Station, 3rd generation, modified) is used, designed for the safe regeneration of an artifact's energy structure. The station creates a controlled field imitating the spectrum of waves typical of the anomaly that produced the artifact, thereby "feeding" the anomalous core without overloading it. It is important to note that each artifact has a limited number of charging cycles, after which its structure becomes unstable or degrades; this requires strict control of the parameters and rules out improvised recharging methods.

### Comparative tests of detectors
Field tests included comparative trials between:

* **improvised detectors**, widespread among stalkers,
* the serial scientific detector **"Otklik"** (Echo).

The results showed:

* improvised detectors register only a narrow band of waves, often reacting only to the background of the anomaly,
* they cannot tell an artifact's quality before it is extracted,
* there is a high risk of missing rare and unusual formations.

The **"Otklik"** detector, by contrast:

* covers a much wider spectrum of wave ranges,
* can tell the type and potential quality of an artifact even *before contact*,
* works steadily in complex anomalous fields

### Influence of the detector on final quality

It has been established experimentally that the extraction method and tool directly affect an artifact's final quality. Incorrect localisation of the wave centre or abrupt interference can:

* disrupt the structure of the anomalous core,
* lower the quality to the "Junk" category,
* completely destroy a potentially valuable object.

The detector is therefore not just a search tool but a key element of the extraction process.

### Conclusion

An artifact is not a static object with predefined properties. Its quality, behaviour and potential are determined by the conditions of its formation, the depth of analysis and, critically, the equipment used to detect and extract it.

In this context the "Otklik" detector should be seen not as an auxiliary device but as a tool for the primary interpretation of an anomaly, without which full scientific and practical work with anomalous formations is impossible; it is, however, worth considering an improved tool for the secondary interpretation of an anomaly.
