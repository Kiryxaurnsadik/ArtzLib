Anomalous fields are relatively small formations of unknown nature. Field, laboratory and theoretical studies show that anomalies are not objects in the classical sense. Instead, they are stable local processes arising from fundamental disruptions of the interaction between matter, energy and space within the Chernobyl Anomalous Zone.

### Behaviour of anomalous fields
Around the so-called "Emission", or energieabschluss, anomalies show the following properties:

* They can change where their effect is located.
* They can disappear for a time and then reappear.
* Their manifestation does not obey standard physical laws, which makes classical methods of description of limited use.

### Classification of anomalies

* **Thermal anomalous fields (TAF)**
  * Zones of spontaneous heat release without an identifiable source. Temperature peaks reach values incompatible with the survival of organic tissue.
* **Electrical anomalous fields (EAF)**
  * Areas of steady accumulation of static electricity with periodic high-power discharges.
* **Chemical anomalous fields (ChAF)**
  * Anomalies in which the stability of chemical compounds and states of matter is disrupted. Stable chemical bonds break down and chemically aggressive environments appear.
    * Subclass ChAF-1: aerosol
    * Subclass ChAF-2: liquid

* **Gravitational-spatial anomalous fields (GSAF)**
  * Expressed as local curvature of space, a change in the direction of gravity and uncontrolled movement of objects.
    * GSAF-1 compression (squeezing).
    * GSAF-2 ejection (repulsion).
    * GSAF-3 interspatial ("teleportation").
* **Air anomalous fields (AAF)**
  * Local disruptions of the normal behaviour of air and objects in it, showing up as sudden gusts, vertical shifts and strange manoeuvres of aircraft. Little studied at present, but similar to GSAF.
* **Psi anomalous fields (PsAF)**
  * Areas of steady psi exposure with pronounced optical and acoustic distortions of the surroundings. They are recognised by a characteristic rippling of space: the air inside the zone seems denser, the outlines of objects blur, and there may be fog-like haze, false silhouettes and visual displacement of landscape features. In this anomalous field a person is subjected to psychotropic pressure of varying intensity. Early symptoms include a growing headache, a feeling of pressure in the temples and disorientation. The effect can appear instantly or build up over time; a long stay sharply increases the risk of severe consequences. With continued exposure, pronounced sensory disturbances are recorded in the form of visual and auditory hallucinations.
* **Radiation anomalous fields (RAF)**
  * Local zones with a suddenly appearing and unstable background radiation far above the natural level. At the edge of the field, instruments record a sharp jump in readings, from 100 to 300 µSv/h, sometimes with short peaks above the base values.

### Classification of anomalous field stability

* Type A (stable): persist for months.
* Type B (quasi-stable): active in cycles.
* Type C (fluctuating): appear and disappear spontaneously.

Examples of type A anomalous fields include interspatial-transfer GSAF; type B, the anomalous fields scattered across the CAZ that exist from one Storm to the next; type C fields are similar to the anomalous storm and GSAF.

### Classification of anomalous field units

* Single: isolated anomalies of one type.
* Conglomerates: a group of anomalies of one type existing close to each other within a certain radius.
* Arch-clusters: a large cluster of anomalies of different types within a certain radius, with peculiar quirks of their own.
