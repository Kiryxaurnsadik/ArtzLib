### General concept of the kit

The design of the field surgical kit is based on the principle of controlled incomplete sterility. Absolute sterility cannot be maintained in the CAZ, so the priority shifts towards:

* instruments that withstand repeated disinfection.
* quick local sterilisation.
* minimising the risk of secondary infection.
* self-sufficiency and compactness.

The kit is designed to be used by a single medic with no access to hospital conditions.

### Bag and design of the carried module

A soft field bag with a rigid inner frame. The outer layer is *rubberised fabric, resistant to moisture and blood, and easy to treat with antiseptics.* The inner material is a light-coloured synthetic fabric (for visual control of contamination) with partial antibacterial impregnation.

The bag is divided into functional sections:

1. **Instrument compartment**

   * fixed elastic holders,
   * the instruments do not touch each other,
   * *non-sterile but disinfected* instruments may be stored here, instead of in trays.

2. **Sterile compartment (inner pockets)**

   * sealed pockets,
   * intended for sterile wipes and dressings,
     opened only at the moment of the procedure.

3. **Quick disinfection compartment**

   * a metal or heat-resistant bowl,
   * used for alcohol, antiseptic or boiling.

### Main surgical instruments

All instruments are made of medical-grade stainless steel, resistant to corrosion and repeated treatment, unlike non-sterile trays of instruments.

The required minimum in the bag:

* a scalpel with replaceable blades.
* surgical scissors (straight).
* haemostatic clamps (2 to 3).
* anatomical forceps and forceps for removing foreign bodies.
* a needle holder.
* a wound retractor (simple).
* a surgical needle (reusable).
* a set of suture material (consumable).

The pocket system is designed to be opened step by step, which reduces the risk of infection.

### Reuse cycle

After each use:

1. Mechanical cleaning of the instrument.
2. Chemical disinfection.
3. Boiling, where possible.
4. Drying and placing back in the instrument compartment.

The minimum acceptable number of cycles is **4**, provided there is no visible damage or corrosion.

### Advantages over trays

* does not require fully sterile conditions,
* adapted to dirty and especially anomalous environments,
* reduces weight thanks to reusable components,
* makes real surgical procedures possible, not just first aid.

### Conclusion

The field surgical kit is a compromise with medical standards in the form of trays of surgical instruments. It is not ideal from the point of view of classical medicine, but in the conditions of the CAZ it is exactly this kind of system that gives the best chance of survival. As one stalker in the CAZ put it, it is about controlling risk and time, and packing your medicine for the road the right way.
