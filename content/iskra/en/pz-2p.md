Operation of first-generation psi helmets showed that they are highly effective against background and diffuse psi radiation, but it also revealed critical limitations:

* excessive size and power consumption.
* inability to counter **directed sources of psi pressure**.
* poor adaptation to changes in radiation intensity.
* a high heat signature and overloading of independent power sources.

The PZ-2 was developed as a compact, energy-efficient and more versatile psi shielding system.

### General concept of the PZ-2
The second-generation psi screen is a portable neuro-electromagnetic shielding system that forms a closed protective biofield shell around the user's head and upper body.

The main idea of the upgrade is the move from passive shielding to active phased protection able to respond to directed psi attacks.

### Design changes to the elements

The PZ-2's component base was completely redesigned:
### Replaced:

* bulky electrode domes → with segmented neuro-induction modules.
* analogue generator units → with a digital phased array.
* heavy batteries → with compact high-density power cells.

### This made it possible to:

* significantly reduce the weight of the device.
* lower power consumption.
* improve the reliability and stability of the protective field.

Despite its smaller size, the device remains an external exoskeletal helmet that cannot be hidden under headgear.
*(see Blueprint No. 1)*

### Active psi protection system

The main innovation of the PZ-2 is an adaptive spiral-type white psi noise generator. Unlike the first generation, which created a static field, the new system forms:

* a spiral multilayer protective signature.
* a phase shift of psi wavefronts.
* dispersion of the directed psi flow.

As a result, protection is achieved not only against:

* background psi radiation.
* residual anomalous effects.

but also against directed psi exposure of medium intensity. The PZ-2's field forms as a dynamic spiral rotating around the wearer's head.

This configuration:

* blurs the focus of the psi beam.
* prevents biofield resonance.
* disperses energy into safe frequencies.

In essence, the device does not block the radiation completely but breaks its coherence, making the exposure harmless.

### Power consumption and autonomy
Thanks to the switch to a new component base:

* power consumption has been more than halved.
* continuous operating time has increased.
* heat output has decreased.
* the load on external power sources has been reduced.

The PZ-2 can be used on long expeditions without constant recharging.

### Remaining drawbacks
Despite significant progress, the PZ-2 retains a number of limitations:

* a bulky design.
* it cannot be worn covertly.
* limited effectiveness against extreme psi events.
* no full protection against highly organised psi entities and emissions.

These drawbacks are considered to be expected for a prototype and are seen as the price of a proven concept.

### Conclusion
The PZ-2 became the first truly practical means of individual protection against directed psi exposure.

It stopped being a laboratory experiment and became a working tool for field research, significantly improving the survival of personnel working in zones of high psi activity.
