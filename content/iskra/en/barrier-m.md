While studying the behaviour of anomalously mutated organisms in the CAZ, it was found that most aggressive life forms have a disrupted central nervous system and an unstable biofield. This applies above all to most of the anomalous fauna of the CAZ, in particular zombified stalkers, snorks and bloodsuckers.

On the basis of earlier research, the scientist S. P. Fidov developed a portable mutant repeller that uses combined psi-resonance neuroacoustic radiation. The device generates directed pulses of a special frequency that act on the damaged areas of mutants' brains, causing disorientation, fear, aggression towards each other or an instinctive urge to leave the area covered by the signal.

For an ordinary person or animals with an undamaged nervous system the effect is practically harmless. In rare cases the following are noted:

* Headache.
* Ringing in the ears.
* A feeling of anxiety.
* Brief disorientation.

### Device design
The device is a compact rectangular metal block with a sloping instrument panel. The housing is made of:

* Aluminium alloy.
* Shielded steel plates.
* An inner copper mesh to stabilise the radiation.

On top of the housing there is a folding dish-type emitter antenna that can change the direction of the signal.

### The front panel contains:

* Analogue power controls.
* A mode switch.
* A charge indicator.
* An emergency pulse button.
* An overheating diagnostic indicator.

### Inside the housing:

* A psi-resonance generator unit.
* A neuroacoustic modulator.
* A pulse converter.
* A shielded battery compartment.
* A cooling system based on radiator plates.

### Operating modes
### 1. Standard mode
The basic repelling mode.
The device emits a weak psi-acoustic field with a radius of up to 25 metres. Most mutants begin to avoid the device's range, lose coordination or abandon the chase.

It is especially effective against:

* blind dogs;
* pseudodogs;
* boars;
* snorks;
* zombies.

Power consumption is low.

### 2. Boosted mode
Increases the power of the pulses and the depth of their effect on the target's nervous system. The range decreases, but the signal intensity rises several times over. Some mutants show:

* Seizures.
* Loss of orientation.
* Temporary paralysis.
* Aggression towards other mutants.

During tests, bloodsuckers under the boosted mode lost their camouflage because their nervous regulation was disrupted. Everything has its drawbacks, and ours are:

* Rapid overheating.
* Increased battery drain.
* Risk of damage to the electronics.

### 3. Experimental "Scorch" mode
A restricted experimental mode. In this state the device generates short high-energy psi pulses aimed at destroying the damaged areas of the target's brain. The operating principle is presumably based in part on technologies used in the installations of the Raduga-1 project. When the effect succeeds, the target suffers:

* destruction of neural connections;
* cessation of motor activity;
* instant death or irreversible brain degradation.

*Mode limitations:*

* a nearly fully charged battery is required;
* severe overheating after 2 or 3 pulses;
* frequency instability;
* it does not work on some forms of mutants.

Particularly low effectiveness has been noted against:

* controllers;
* pseudogiants;
* poltergeists.

This is presumably due to the different structure of their nervous systems and the presence of their own anomalous biofields.

### Theory of the effect
According to my research and my own observations, mutants' brains contain areas of anomalously altered tissue that act as natural amplifiers of psi activity and cause their aggressive behaviour. The device creates a resonance with these areas, producing certain symptoms and effects such as:

* Overload of the nervous system.
* Pain impulses.
* Impaired orientation.
* A panic reaction.

Zombified stalkers are especially vulnerable because of the destruction of the higher brain regions and an unstable biofield.

### Drawbacks of the device
Despite its effectiveness, the device has a number of serious drawbacks:

* The signal is easy to detect for some psi-active mutants.
* Limited battery life.
* Batteries need frequent replacement.
* Sensitivity to electromagnetic anomalies.
* Risk of reverse resonance when operating near psi fields.

During tests near psi anomalies the following cases were recorded:

* Spontaneous activation.
* Generator overload.
* Uncontrolled expansion of the range.

### Conclusion
The portable mutant repeller, which we had better call Barrier-M, became one of the first successful examples of mobile psi-acting equipment for field use. Despite its instability and heavy power demands, the device proved that mutants can be suppressed locally without direct firefights.
